# Nigeria vehicle import basics: practical guides for buyers and importers

Status: `DRAFT` | Language: English | Market: Nigeria | Updated: 2 September 2026

These articles are plain-language summaries for cross-border vehicle buyers. They are not a customs ruling, tax opinion or registration guarantee.

## 1. Before importing a vehicle to Nigeria, confirm the current restriction for the exact VIN

### The short answer

Vehicle imports must be declared to the Nigeria Customs Service (NCS) through an approved port and customs process. The importer, vehicle description, VIN, origin and intended use determine the documents, valuation and charges. Prohibited or restricted categories can change, so a supplier's “Nigeria accepted” claim is not enough.

### Steps before purchase

1. Confirm the Nigerian importer and licensed customs agent responsible for the declaration.
2. Check the VIN, first registration date, steering configuration, engine or motor type and condition history.
3. Ask the agent to check the current NCS import prohibition/restriction list and tariff treatment.
4. Confirm the destination port or terminal and who bears demurrage, storage and examination costs.
5. Put the agreed vehicle specification and document list into the purchase contract.

### Main risks

An incorrect VIN, under-declared value, wrong HS code or restricted vehicle can trigger examination, reassessment, delay or seizure action under the applicable rules. Do not promise road registration until customs and the relevant registration authorities have accepted the vehicle.

Official starting point: [Nigeria Customs Service](https://customs.gov.ng/). Use the live NCS tariff and import-guideline pages for the declaration date.

## 2. Nigeria customs clearance: keep the declaration trail together

### The short answer

The shipping and sales records must tell the same story. The customs agent uses the bill of lading, invoice, vehicle identity and importer records to prepare the Single Goods Declaration and obtain the customs assessment and release steps required at the selected port.

### Typical records to prepare

1. Bill of lading or sea waybill with the correct consignee and VIN.
2. Commercial invoice or sale contract showing price, currency, seller and buyer.
3. Export certificate, foreign title or registration record, where applicable.
4. VIN and full technical description: make, model, year, fuel or power source, engine capacity and condition.
5. Importer identification, tax details and the authorised customs agent's information.
6. Any standards, inspection, insurance or port documents requested by NCS or the terminal.

### What happens next

The agent files the declaration, Customs reviews the classification and value, and the vehicle may be selected for examination. Release follows only after assessed amounts are paid and any hold is cleared. Road registration is a separate process.

### Warning

Ask for the declaration reference and an itemised assessment. Do not send extra “release” money to an unknown intermediary without a verifiable terminal or customs receipt.

Official starting points: [NCS](https://customs.gov.ng/) and [Nigerian Ports Authority](https://nigerianports.gov.ng/). Confirm the live checklist with the appointed agent.

## 3. Nigeria landed cost: separate customs charges from port and agent fees

### The short answer

The final cost can include customs duty and statutory levies, VAT or other taxes, and private charges such as terminal handling, examination, storage, demurrage, agency and inland transport. The amount depends on the declared customs value, HS code, vehicle category, origin and the rules in force when the declaration is assessed.

### Build the estimate in five lines

1. Vehicle price and freight/insurance data used for the customs value.
2. HS classification and NCS tariff treatment.
3. Duty, VAT and statutory levies shown separately.
4. Terminal, inspection, storage and demurrage assumptions with dates.
5. Agency and inland-delivery fees, with the validity period of the quote.

Do not publish one percentage as “Nigeria import tax”. A quote must be recalculated when the vehicle, origin, exchange rate or declaration date changes.

Official starting point: [NCS tariff and customs resources](https://customs.gov.ng/). The NCS assessment and a licensed Nigerian customs agent should control the final customer quote.
